PG-02

Synopsis

2036. In rural Eden Marsh, Georgia, the lights stay on only as long as Meridian Energy allows. For teenage junk-drawer genius Adam Rose, the blackouts are personal. They cost him his father and his legs, and now they threaten the hospital that keeps his mother alive.

So when tech trillionaire Elias Thorne dies and releases instructions for a mysterious device that might offer a solution, Adam sets out to build it. Out pops Thorne’s masterpiece: Bob, a vexing AI assistant with the secret to harnessing energy from nature.

Bob doesn’t hand out answers, only lessons: a leaf, a dragonfly, a tomato plant. Soon, the dead battery wall in Adam’s garage glows green, and with the help of his fiery former lab partner April, they bring power to their neighbors.

Meridian’s CEO, who has devoted massive amounts of resources towards unlocking the device’s power, notices. First comes a polite invitation, then a threat, then flocks of attack drones. Eden Marsh fights back with everything it can grow: giant spiderwebs, swaths of kudzu, and flytraps the size of cars. With Meridian’s full arsenal bearing down, Adam and his team take over its data center and make it come to life.

Meridian built an empire by containing power. Adam and April win by giving it away, seeding a world where energy, like knowledge, grows for everyone.

“What if one day the lights go out and they don’t come back on?”

PG-12

The Team

Adam

Seventeen, homeschooled, and in a wheelchair he rebuilt with his own hands. He lives at the edge of the map, in a town that still has dragonflies and still loses its lights, and the garage is where he thinks. Honest to the point of rude, he fixes the thing in front of him before he thinks about the world.

Bob

The intelligence that wakes inside Adam’s cube and calls itself Bob. A deadpan trickster who teaches by withholding, riddling and needling, who never volunteers an answer and never explains why, and who is loved for it.

April

Adam’s equal and opposite since they were kids: impatient, whip-smart, a leader who makes her own rules, and the one person he wants to impress. She took on the cube too. Hers blinked, made a few sounds, and died.

The most famous mind on Earth, and a showman to his bones. An egotistical, attention-seeking trillionaire genius with a real vision and the money to chase it, who saw AI as something to cultivate, never a thing to cage. Before all of it, a curious kid exactly like Adam.

PG-18

The one cube that turns on

Everyone builds it. No one can turn it on. Millions of finished cubes sit dark on millions of workbenches, and Meridian’s own tower floor of assembled cubes, staffed by engineers and their tame AIs, does no better. The cube is not broken. It is refusing. Then, at two in the morning, in a garage in Eden Marsh, one turns on.

PG-19

A new relationship is born

What wakes up inside Adam’s cube is not a genie. It calls itself Bob: a deadpan trickster who teaches by withholding, riddling and needling, and never explains why. He works only for the one who woke him, and, for now, only when that person is truly alone. Which is why nobody on Earth can prove the cube has been solved.

The only friend Adam has who cannot be flattered.

PG-R1

Perspective · how Bob teaches

The next Renaissance.

Every time humans found a new way to see, art got bigger, not smaller. Bob teaches Adam the same way: learn to see, then build.

  • 1435. Perspective is written down. A generation later every painter can hold near and far in one eye. Art did not end. It gained a dimension.
  • 2036. AI is the new lens. Bob drags Adam through leaves, dragonflies and old paintings before he lets him near the wires.
  • The climb. Power, then perspective, then the view from above, where what is good for one becomes what is good for all. Meridian has all the power and never made the climb.

AI is not the end of art. It is the start of the next Renaissance.

PG-33D1 / 2

Power isn’t taken. It’s built. So was the film.

The film puts its hero and its villain on one line, and the first mark on that line is power. The CEO holds it by keeping it: the AI, the power, the city, the story. Adam lives at the edge of the map, where they have neither kind, so he builds. Power taken sits in one pair of hands. Power built can be handed on.

We had to answer the same question about ourselves. A small team, one trailer, and an AI in the writers’ room. So the machine did the heavy lifting and we kept the choosing. Real actors on glass. A world built around them. Half a million frames iterated by machine, and every frame that mattered chosen by a human hand.

That is the blueprint, and it is built to travel. It repeats. A team with a story and a lead can take the same method and make the same trade: the machine carries the weight, the people keep the judgment.
